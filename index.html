How bidirectional search finds a route

  1. Two searches start

    One search leaves the start and one leaves the goal. Each spreads outward, the most promising cells first.

  2. The frontiers meet

    The first contact gives a real route from start to goal. A shorter one may still exist.

  3. The search closes the gap

    A lower bound climbs towards the best route found. While the two numbers differ, a cheaper route is still possible.

  4. The shortest route is proven

    When the numbers match, the search stops. The route on the map is the shortest one there is.

    Questions about the search

    Why not stop when the two sides meet?

    The first meeting gives a route, often not the shortest. Stopping there would give wrong answers on maps with walls or mud.

    What is a frontier?

    The cells a search has reached but not yet finished. They are the bright edge on the map.

    What is the estimate?

    A quick guess of the distance left, such as Manhattan distance. A safe estimate never guesses too high.

    What is the lower bound?

    A number the search has proved no route can beat. The search ends when the best route reaches it.

    Does it need an install or an account?

    No. Everything runs in this page, and your maps stay in your browser.
